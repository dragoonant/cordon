# How to Play CORDON

## Squads and Rows

Your basic unit is a **squad**: up to 4 mechs in a 2×3 formation. **Front row** mechs absorb hits and use close-range weapons. **Back row** mechs stay protected, fire from range, or heal. Every weapon has row behavior — a lance works great in front (rushes forward), useless in back; a railgun shines in back (precise), but is rushed and inaccurate up front. Squad composition matters: a squad of four front-row mechs is vulnerable to ranged fire.

## Pilots and Growth

Pilots are people. Mechs are hardware. **Pilots grow** by fighting; mechs don't. Each pilot has five **Aptitudes** (0–100, grown by use):
- **Gunnery** — ranged accuracy and critical hit chance
- **Melee** — close-range damage and counter attacks
- **Evasion** — dodge
- **Systems** — part efficiency, sensor range, forecast accuracy
- **Command** — squad morale, leader bonuses, Nerve regeneration

As aptitudes grow, pilots unlock **Certifications** — new specializations that unlock new frame weights and callouts. A veteran pilot can eventually fly superheavy frames; a rookie can only handle light ones.

## Nerve and Callouts

Every pilot has a **Nerve pool** (capped per pilot). Callouts are powerful pre-battle and overworld abilities that cost Nerve to use. Nerve regenerates: **+1 every 20 seconds deployed**, **+5 when an objective completes**, **+8 to survivors when a squadmate dies**, and **+3 to winners** — the game's comeback engine, since the worst moments pay out the most. Strategic Nerve use is the core of CORDON: you never have enough to use everything. Every Nerve bar in the UI (roster, squad panel, hangar) explains this on hover, and spending Nerve flashes the bar with a "-N NERVE" readout.

### Pre-Battle Callouts (on the Forecast screen)

**Eyes On** — Reveals the enemy's full loadout and formation. The forecast becomes exact — no variance band. (Costs 4 Nerve; requires Marksman certification.)

**Break Formation** — Your squad ignores row rules this battle. Back row can melee, front row can fire. (Costs 3 Nerve; squad loses defensive row bonus.)

**Redline It** — Your mech deals +60% damage this battle. (Costs 5 Nerve; frame takes permanent structural damage for the run — max HP loss you'll feel later.)

**On Me** — You draw every attack aimed at a chosen squadmate this battle. (Costs 3 Nerve; you eat the hits meant for them.)

**Punch Out** — If your mech is destroyed this battle, you eject safely — guaranteed. (Costs 2 Nerve; requires Field Tech certification; mech is lost either way.)

**First One's Mine** — You attack first, before the enemy's opening round, regardless of speed. (Costs 3 Nerve; requires Vanguard certification; your evasion drops to zero for round one.)

**Chain It** — The first kill this battle grants every squadmate a free follow-up attack. (Costs 5 Nerve; requires Marksman certification; snowball play.)

**Sell It** — The enemy targets the wrong row for round one. (Costs 3 Nerve; requires Wing Lead certification; a feint, not a buff.)

**We Hold** — Your squad cannot be routed this battle and fights every round to the end. Survivors gain permanent morale. (Costs 4 Nerve; requires Vanguard certification; nobody retreats — including the ones who should.)

### Overworld Callouts (on the real-time map)

**Burn Hard** — Your squad moves at 2× speed for 30 seconds. (Costs 3 Nerve; requires Recon certification; fuel drains 3× and you must idle 15s afterward.)

**Ping The Sector** — Reveal all enemy squads and headings in a radius for 20 seconds. (Costs 4 Nerve; requires Recon certification; enemies in that radius are alerted to you in return.)

**Rally The Channel** — All friendly squads in range recover morale. (Costs 3 Nerve; requires Wing Lead certification; your own squad loses morale — they gave it away.)

**Fall Back** — Instant disengage to the nearest friendly point, ignoring pursuit. (Costs 4 Nerve; every mech in your squad takes a damage tick. Escaping costs.)

**Come Get Some** — Enemy squads in range are drawn to your squad for 20 seconds. (Costs 5 Nerve; bait for protecting a rescue — you'd better win.)

**Stay With Them** — Attach your squad to a convoy or evac target; you move together and it takes no damage while you live. (Costs 6 Nerve; you can't disengage until the escort completes.)

## Last Transmissions

When a **pilot dies**, they trigger a unique **Last Transmission** — one final ability that fires automatically, for free. It's not a punishment; it's a moment. Here are the kinds you'll unlock:

- **Don't Stop** — Every friendly squad on the map gains 2× speed for 30 seconds and full Nerve. The map keeps moving.
- **Marking Them** — The squad that killed this pilot is permanently revealed and takes +30% damage from all sources for the run.
- **Take The Frame** — This pilot's mech survives destruction as a recoverable wreck — frame and all three slots salvageable.
- **Got The Shot** — This pilot's final attack fires anyway, at full power, ignoring evasion.
- **Hold Them Here** — The enemy squad that killed this pilot is frozen in place on the map for 20 seconds.
- **Tell Them** — Every pilot's bonds with the deceased convert to a permanent aptitude gain.
- **Light It Up** — All enemy squads on the map are revealed for the rest of the map.
- **Go Home** — Every friendly squad's fuel is refilled.

## Hangar: Frames, Weapons, and Systems

A **mech** is a **frame** (the chassis — a complete named machine with HP, armor, evasion, weight class, mobility rating) plus three slots:

| Slot | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Weapon A** | Lance (front row, 2-hit rush), Assault rifle (flexible), Railgun (back row, precise), Shield-blade (front, counters) |
| **Weapon B** | Missile pod, Vulcans, Grapple, Flare launcher (support) |
| **System** | Shield generator, Booster, ECM jammer, Repair drone, Recon array, Ejection assist |

**Weight vs. Generator:** total slot weight must stay under the frame's generator output or you lose evasion and speed. That tension — packing good gear vs. staying mobile — is the whole build game in one number.

**Frame families:** Light (fast, fragile), Medium (backbone), Heavy (slow, tons of HP), Aerospace (rare, works in both space and ground). **Salvaged Compact frames** (rare enemy drops) are excellent and heavy.

**Certifications gate frames:** a rookie can only fly light frames with an Ejection assist. As they grow, they unlock medium, then heavy, then superheavy. Pilots also unlock specializations: **Vanguard** (melee bonuses), **Marksman** (ranged bonuses), **Field Tech** (repairs mid-battle), **Recon** (map vision, speed), **Wing Lead** (squad buffs).

## Mobility Rating

Every frame has one: **Space**, **Ground**, or **Aerospace**. Maps are either space (debris fields, orbital stations) or ground (forests, cities, mountains). A space-rated frame in gravity is sluggish and loses evasion. A ground frame in space is clumsy and slow. Aerospace frames work both ways — they're rare, light-armored, and the key to a flexible hangar. Routes are visible one sector ahead, so plan your frames for what's coming.

## Salvage and Scrap

When enemy mechs are destroyed, they drop **weapons and systems** frequently, and **whole frames** rarely. You also find salvage at dedicated Salvage nodes. Everything is useful or tradeable for scrap — the currency you use to repair damaged mechs, buy frames at depots, or upgrade items. Salvage is how you grow your hangar between maps.

## Standing and Ascension

**Standing** measures how the rescued colonies regard you. It goes up when you complete rescues, down when you fail them. High standing at run's end unlocks rewards and new pilots. After you beat the run, **Ascension modifiers** stack — difficulty settings that unlock progressively (rival appears earlier, salvage drops less, Nerve regenerates slower, etc.). The game gets harder each clear if you want it to.

## Bonds

Pilots who fight in the same squad build **bonds**. At milestones, they unlock **Tandem Callouts** — powerful two-pilot abilities that cost both their Nerve but pack extra punch:

- **Cross-Fire** — Two back-row pilots fire simultaneously at one target: combined damage, guaranteed hit.
- **Switch** — Front and back pilots swap rows mid-battle after round one, wasting the enemy's targeting.
- **Got Your Six** — Any attack that would kill Pilot A is intercepted by Pilot B at half damage. Once per battle.
- **Double Time** — The pair's mechs both act twice in round one.

Bonds reset each run, but the story moments they unlock persist.
